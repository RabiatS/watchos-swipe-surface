Good evening, and thank you for being here.

Tonight I want to talk about a small thing on the wrist and the large thing it might do.

We have spent ten years teaching watches to measure us. Steps, sleep, heart rate, oxygen. Every sensor pointed inward.

This project points the watch the other way. It treats the screen on the wrist as a control surface for the screens around us. The phone on the stand. The television across the room. The headset with no buttons at all.

Five gestures. Up, down, left, right, and a tap. That is the whole vocabulary. The watch tells you what each one will do before you do it.

You are reading this from a teleprompter driven by exactly that. If I need to slow down, I flick down. If I lose my place, I flick left and the words come back.

Nothing here is new on its own. What is new is treating the watch as a device you hold rather than a device you wear.

Thank you.
